# Demo auto claim handling procedure

DEMO / SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT — NOT A REAL INSURANCE CONTRACT

This invented internal procedure demonstrates RAG for auto claims across demo products. It is not a real insurer workflow, French insurance standard, or legal guidance.

## Initial Completeness Check

At intake, check the claim identifier, policy product, incident date, vehicle details, declared damage, claim form, collision account, and repair estimate. Compare these entries with the applicable demo policy evidence requirements. Record each item as present, missing, or needing clarification. This check does not decide coverage.

## Missing Documents

When evidence is missing, record the specific missing items and send a targeted request to the claimant. Mark the dossier as awaiting documents and record the follow-up request. Do not record the missing item as received and do not automatically reject coverage merely because a document is missing. Resume the completeness check when the material arrives.

## Human and Expert Review

Request human review when documents contradict the claim's incident, vehicle, or damage description, or when the applicable demo policy does not clearly address the question. Request a vehicle repair expert when the estimate includes unexplained repair items or the damage mechanism is unclear. Automated extraction or a similarity score alone must not decide coverage.

## Escalation Steps

First record the unresolved issue and supporting document references. Next refer it to a claims supervisor. The supervisor assigns a policy specialist for unclear terms or a repair expert for technical damage questions. Keep the case pending review and record the assigned reviewer and next action. Escalation is a request for review, not an adverse decision.

## Decision Documentation

Record the decision rationale, applicable policy source ID and version, cited sections, evidence reviewed, outstanding limitations, and the human reviewer. Record the review date and follow-up actions. A final decision must be traceable to the documents used; a generated RAG answer is supporting information for the reviewer.
